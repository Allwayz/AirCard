# Visual research notes

The bundled skins are original artwork. These links were used only as visual/historical references; their images are **not redistributed** in this repository.

## Transit references

- **London / Oyster** — Transport for London notes Oyster was introduced in 2003 and describes the blue-and-white card as a protected, instantly recognisable design:
  - https://madeby.tfl.gov.uk/2025/01/27/tfl-25-years/
  - https://content.tfl.gov.uk/tfl-ip-guidance-doc.pdf
  - Historical photograph: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oystercard.jpg
- **Hong Kong / Octopus** — Octopus officially launched in September 1997. Octopus also operates a first-party Card Image Store for mobile cards:
  - https://www.octopus.com.hk/about-us/corporate-profile/our-history/en/index.html
  - https://www.octopus.com.hk/en/consumer/mobile-payment/card-image-store/about/index.html
- **Paris / Navigo** — Île-de-France Mobilités states the Navigo visual changed in 2014 with a design by Philippe Starck:
  - https://www.iledefrance-mobilites.fr/en/navigo-une-carte-securisee
- **New York / MetroCard** — MTA material shows the familiar yellow/blue MetroCard visual language:
  - https://www.mta.info/document/586
- **Seoul / T-money** — T-money's 2026 basic black stripe card uses a matte black base with subtle stripe pattern:
  - https://www.tmoney.co.kr/aeb/cmnctn/news/readNewsView.dev?bdSeqNo=4070

Additional visual references used during the design study included current photographs of Singapore EZ-Link, JR East Suica, and Sydney Opal cards. No source photograph or logo artwork is embedded in the generated PNGs.

## Design policy

The goal of this library is **recognisable regional mood without one-to-one copying**:

- do not copy serial numbers or real account data;
- do not reproduce official issuer logos unless you have permission;
- prefer original geometry, typography, colour studies and city motifs;
- keep third-party names in research metadata rather than pretending the skins are official products.
